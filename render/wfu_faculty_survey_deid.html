New developments

I. Enhanced Access to Digital Resources & Tools:

  • Performing Arts: A strong demand exists for institutional subscriptions to high-quality streaming services for live theatre and dance, similar to “Digital Theatre plus” and “National Theatre at Home.”

  • Research Databases & Journals: Faculty in Finance and Economics specifically request access to more leading journals and relevant databases, as well as economics blogs (e.g., on Substack).

  • Archival Access: There’s a desire for expanded access to British newspaper archives, such as The London Times and Spectator.

  • Software & Platforms: Requests include:

  • Overleaf Premium Subscription: To provide a university-wide license, similar to existing departmental ones.

  • BioRender License: For creating scientific figures and illustrations.

  • Tableau & NVivo: Access to data visualization and qualitative data analysis software, potentially through institutional licenses or virtual labs.

  • AI Tools: Interest in a “Plus version for ChatGPT” suggests a need for advanced AI capabilities for research and productivity.

II. Support for Digital Scholarship & Research Methodologies:

  • Digital Humanities: This is a significant area, with requests for:

  • Training in identifying and using useful tools for text analysis, mapping, network analysis, and TEI coding.

  • General “Digital Humanities training.”

  • Research Workflow & Transparency:

  • Support for pre-registering studies in psychology, indicating a move towards more transparent and rigorous research practices.

  • Guidance on systematic review tools, as the technology in this area is rapidly improving. The library could help researchers stay informed of the latest tools.

  • Data Management & Visualization:

  • New data visualization tools, tips, and techniques.

  • An introductory GIS workshop is explicitly requested.

  • Need for enhanced cloud storage to support research data.

  • Transcription Services: Programs/software that can transcribe audio files are needed across disciplines, particularly in music for score cleaning and historical document transcription, and for language transcriptions.

III. Capacity Building & Skill Development:

  • Respondents express a need for support in developing skills for creating online experiences (courses, content presentation), including guidance on sharing and organizing digital information. This highlights a need for training and confidence-building in digital pedagogy and content creation.

  • The request for help with “Norwegian Art Song Anthology” (music discipline) underscores a need for specialized support in dealing with historical documents, score cleaning, and language transcriptions, indicating a broader need for discipline-specific expertise and resources within the library.

Additional comments

Positive Feedback & Staff Recognition


  • Overwhelmingly positive sentiment; feeling “super supported” and well-served.

  • Subject librarians lauded for purchases and course support.

  • Specific shout-outs to Colleen Foy, Hu, Tanya, and Kathy Shields for exceptional help with data, archives, and purchases.

  • ILL (Interlibrary Loan) described as a “godsend.”

  • Library appreciated as a productive change of pace/environment.

Resource & Access Requests


  • More digital content: German newspapers, French psychology and humanities journals, deeper digital theatre and dance archives.

  • Increased budget: For books and journals.

  • Physical holdings: Desire for more physical books and greater room/access for them.

  • New software/tools: Subscription to EndNote, resources for AI tools.

  • Newspaper archives: More access, currently self-funding.

Areas for Improvement & Collaboration


  • Website design: Needs to be more research-focused, less geared toward undergraduates.

  • Software access/training: Tutorials/workshops needed for existing software (e.g., MaxQDA) to increase awareness and usability; issues with MaxQDA VPN reliance and collaboration.

  • Collaboration: Work with WFU IS and CAT to capture expertise and prevent redundancy.

  • Database organization: Negative feedback on new database organization; missed easy access to encyclopedias, WorldCat, and major general databases on the homepage.

  • Advocacy: Suggestion for library involvement in academic freedom and resisting book banning.

  • Facilitation: Even if not providing services directly, act as a facilitator for finding external services.
